# Legal Complaint Format Research

## Summary

Federal class action complaints for vehicle defects follow a structured format governed by the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, particularly Rule 23. CaseRadar will generate draft complaints that follow this structure while allowing attorney customization.

## Section Details

### 1. Caption Format

```
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT
[DISTRICT NAME]

─────────────────────────────────────────
[PLAINTIFF NAME], individually and on    )
behalf of all others similarly situated, )
                                         )   Case No. ____________
              Plaintiff,                 )
                                         )   CLASS ACTION COMPLAINT
         v.                              )
                                         )   JURY TRIAL DEMANDED
[DEFENDANT NAME],                        )
                                         )
              Defendant.                 )
─────────────────────────────────────────
```

### 2. Introduction

**Purpose**: Hook the reader, summarize the case in 2-3 paragraphs.

**Template**:
```
1. This is a class action brought on behalf of all persons who purchased or
leased [VEHICLE YEAR RANGE] [MAKE] [MODEL] vehicles equipped with [COMPONENT]
that are defective and prone to [FAILURE MODE].

2. Defendant [MANUFACTURER] knew or should have known of this defect through
consumer complaints, warranty claims, and internal testing, yet failed to
disclose the defect to consumers or remedy it in a timely manner.

3. As a result of this defect, Plaintiff and Class Members have suffered
[DAMAGES TYPE] including [SPECIFIC DAMAGES].
```

### 3. Jurisdiction and Venue

**CAFA Requirements** (28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)):
- Aggregate amount in controversy > $5,000,000
- Minimal diversity (at least one plaintiff and one defendant from different states)
- Class of 100 or more members

**Template**:
```
JURISDICTION AND VENUE

4. This Court has subject matter jurisdiction pursuant to the Class Action
Fairness Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(2), because: (a) there are 100 or more
Class Members; (b) the aggregate amount in controversy exceeds $5,000,000,
exclusive of interest and costs; and (c) at least one Class Member is a
citizen of a state different from Defendant.

5. This Court has personal jurisdiction over Defendant because Defendant
conducts substantial business in this District, and the acts giving rise
to this Complaint occurred in this District.

6. Venue is proper in this District pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2)
because a substantial part of the events giving rise to Plaintiff's claims
occurred in this District.
```

### 4. Parties

**Plaintiff Template**:
```
7. Plaintiff [NAME] is a citizen of [STATE], residing in [CITY], [STATE].
Plaintiff purchased/leased a [YEAR] [MAKE] [MODEL] on or about [DATE] from
[DEALER NAME] in [CITY], [STATE]. Plaintiff's vehicle is equipped with
[DEFECTIVE COMPONENT].
```

**Defendant Template**:
```
8. Defendant [FULL CORPORATE NAME] is a [STATE OF INCORPORATION] corporation
with its principal place of business in [CITY], [STATE]. Defendant designs,
manufactures, markets, distributes, and sells vehicles throughout the
United States, including in this District.
```

### 5. Factual Allegations

**Key Subsections**:

A. **The Defect**
```
THE [COMPONENT] DEFECT

9. [MAKE] [MODEL] vehicles manufactured between [YEARS] are equipped with
[COMPONENT] that is defective in design and/or manufacture.

10. The defect causes [SPECIFIC FAILURE MODE], which can result in
[CONSEQUENCES - e.g., loss of vehicle control, fire, crash].

11. [TECHNICAL EXPLANATION OF DEFECT].
```

B. **Defendant's Knowledge**
```
DEFENDANT'S KNOWLEDGE OF THE DEFECT

12. Defendant knew or should have known of the [COMPONENT] defect through
multiple sources, including but not limited to:

    a. Pre-production testing and design analysis;
    b. Early consumer complaints to Defendant's customer service;
    c. Warranty claims data;
    d. Complaints filed with the National Highway Traffic Safety
       Administration ("NHTSA");
    e. Technical Service Bulletins issued to dealers;
    f. Internal communications and engineering analyses.

13. According to NHTSA records, consumers have filed at least [NUMBER]
complaints regarding [COMPONENT] failures in [MAKE] [MODEL] vehicles.
```

C. **Consumer Harm** (CaseRadar data)
```
CONSUMER COMPLAINTS AND HARM

14. NHTSA complaint data reveals a pattern of [COMPONENT] failures in
[MAKE] [MODEL] vehicles:

    a. Total complaints: [NUMBER]
    b. Complaints involving crashes: [NUMBER]
    c. Complaints involving fires: [NUMBER]
    d. Reported injuries: [NUMBER]
    e. Reported fatalities: [NUMBER]

15. Representative consumer complaints include:

    [COMPLAINT EXCERPTS - anonymized, from NHTSA data]
```

### 6. Class Allegations

**Rule 23(a) Requirements**:

```
CLASS ALLEGATIONS

16. Plaintiff brings this action on behalf of himself and all others
similarly situated pursuant to Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 23(a),
23(b)(2), and 23(b)(3).

17. The proposed Class is defined as:

    All persons who purchased or leased a [YEAR RANGE] [MAKE] [MODEL]
    vehicle in the United States equipped with [DEFECTIVE COMPONENT].

18. Excluded from the Class are: (a) Defendant and its officers, directors,
and employees; (b) any entity in which Defendant has a controlling interest;
(c) the judge(s) assigned to this case; and (d) the legal representatives,
heirs, successors, and assigns of any such excluded person.

NUMEROSITY (Rule 23(a)(1))

19. The Class is so numerous that joinder of all members is impracticable.
Upon information and belief, the Class consists of [tens/hundreds of]
thousands of individuals throughout the United States.

COMMONALITY (Rule 23(a)(2))

20. There are questions of law and fact common to the Class, including:
    a. Whether the [COMPONENT] is defective;
    b. Whether Defendant knew of the defect;
    c. Whether Defendant had a duty to disclose the defect;
    d. Whether Defendant's conduct was negligent;
    e. Whether Class Members are entitled to damages.

TYPICALITY (Rule 23(a)(3))

21. Plaintiff's claims are typical of the claims of the Class because
Plaintiff and all Class Members were injured by the same wrongful conduct.

ADEQUACY (Rule 23(a)(4))

22. Plaintiff will fairly and adequately protect the interests of the Class.
Plaintiff has retained counsel experienced in class action litigation.

PREDOMINANCE AND SUPERIORITY (Rule 23(b)(3))

23. Common questions of law and fact predominate over questions affecting
only individual Class Members.

24. A class action is superior to other methods for adjudication because
the prosecution of separate actions would create the risk of inconsistent
judgments and would be inefficient.
```

### 7. Causes of Action

**Common Counts for Vehicle Defect Cases**:

```
COUNT I - NEGLIGENCE

25. Plaintiff incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs.

26. Defendant had a duty to exercise reasonable care in the design,
manufacture, and sale of its vehicles.

27. Defendant breached this duty by designing, manufacturing, and selling
vehicles with defective [COMPONENT].

28. As a direct and proximate result of Defendant's negligence, Plaintiff
and Class Members have suffered damages.

─────────────────────────────────────────

COUNT II - STRICT PRODUCTS LIABILITY (Design Defect)

29. Plaintiff incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs.

30. The [COMPONENT] in the Class Vehicles is defective in design because
[EXPLANATION].

31. The defect renders the vehicles unreasonably dangerous.

32. The Class Vehicles were used in a manner reasonably foreseeable to
Defendant.

33. The defect was a proximate cause of Plaintiff's and Class Members' damages.

─────────────────────────────────────────

COUNT III - BREACH OF IMPLIED WARRANTY OF MERCHANTABILITY

34. Plaintiff incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs.

35. Defendant impliedly warranted that the Class Vehicles were merchantable
and fit for their ordinary purpose of safe transportation.

36. The Class Vehicles are not merchantable because [DEFECT].

37. As a result, Plaintiff and Class Members have suffered damages.

─────────────────────────────────────────

COUNT IV - FRAUDULENT CONCEALMENT

38. Plaintiff incorporates by reference all preceding paragraphs.

39. Defendant knew of the [COMPONENT] defect but failed to disclose it
to consumers.

40. Defendant had a duty to disclose the defect because [REASONS].

41. Plaintiff and Class Members would not have purchased the vehicles,
or would have paid less, had they known of the defect.

42. Plaintiff and Class Members are entitled to compensatory and punitive
damages.
```

### 8. Prayer for Relief

```
PRAYER FOR RELIEF

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff, on behalf of himself and all others similarly
situated, respectfully requests that this Court:

A. Certify this case as a class action under Rule 23;

B. Appoint Plaintiff as Class Representative;

C. Appoint Plaintiff's counsel as Class Counsel;

D. Award compensatory damages to Plaintiff and the Class;

E. Award punitive damages to Plaintiff and the Class;

F. Award restitution and disgorgement of profits;

G. Award injunctive relief requiring Defendant to repair or replace
   the defective [COMPONENT];

H. Award pre-judgment and post-judgment interest;

I. Award attorneys' fees and costs; and

J. Grant such other relief as the Court deems just and proper.
```

### 9. Jury Demand

```
DEMAND FOR JURY TRIAL

Plaintiff demands a trial by jury on all claims so triable.
```

### 10. Signature Block

```
Dated: [DATE]

                              Respectfully submitted,

                              [FIRM NAME]

                              By: _____________________________
                                  [ATTORNEY NAME]
                                  [BAR NUMBER]
                                  [ADDRESS]
                                  [PHONE]
                                  [EMAIL]

                              Attorneys for Plaintiff and the
                              Proposed Class
```

## PDF Generation Requirements

### Formatting Standards
- **Paper size**: Letter (8.5" x 11")
- **Margins**: 1" all sides
- **Font**: Times New Roman, 12pt (or court-specific)
- **Line spacing**: Double-spaced
- **Paragraph numbering**: Sequential throughout
- **Page numbers**: Bottom center

### Document Structure
- Cover page (caption only)
- Table of contents (for longer complaints)
- Body text
- Signature page

## Recommendations

### MVP Implementation
1. Start with **template-based generation** for structure
2. Use **LLM (Claude) for narrative sections** (Introduction, Factual Allegations)
3. Pull **statistics directly from pattern data**
4. Allow **attorney review and editing** before finalization

### Disclaimer
Generated complaints are **drafts for attorney review**. Include:
```
DRAFT - FOR ATTORNEY REVIEW ONLY
Generated by CaseRadar on [DATE]
This document requires legal review before filing.
```
